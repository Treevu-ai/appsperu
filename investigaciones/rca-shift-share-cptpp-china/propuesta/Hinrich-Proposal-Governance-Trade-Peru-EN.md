## 2. Synopsis of the proposed research

**Trade-related theme.** The project sits squarely in **trade governance** — the Foundation's first priority area. It asks whether the institutional environment of a trading partner is associated with the export performance of Peruvian firms in that market, once the mechanical effects of world demand and product composition are removed. It also connects to **industrial competitiveness and economic development** (Peru's concentration in primary commodities) and to the trade-governance agenda of the **CPTPP**, one of the world's largest trade agreements, of which Peru is a founding signatory alongside ten other economies (the United Kingdom acceded in 2023).

**Context.** Peru's exports doubled between 2015 and 2024 (US$37.4bn → US$76.8bn), yet the country's average governance score moved in the opposite direction — from −0.02 in 2019 to −0.29 in 2024 on the WGI scale, placing Peru around the 27th percentile globally on control of corruption and the 36th on rule of law. Export performance and institutional quality have thus been diverging, not converging. Did Peru gain real market share — competitiveness — or did it merely ride world demand and a favourable product mix? And is the "real competitiveness" component associated with the governance of its trading partners? The existing literature produces contradictory results that depend critically on research design: large-sample gravity studies find robust positive effects of institutional quality on trade (De Groot et al. 2004; Abreo Villamizar et al. 2021), while small-panel studies do not (Riker 2022).

**Key research questions (3):**

1. How much of Peru's 2015–2024 export growth comes from world demand, product-mix structure, and real competitiveness, respectively (shift-share decomposition)?
2. Is the institutional quality of the destination market (Worldwide Governance Indicators) associated with Peru's real export performance and bilateral export volumes — and does the answer change with the statistical design (12-country CPTPP+China panel vs. a world gravity model with 184 partner countries)?
3. Which of the six WGI dimensions shows the strongest association, and does the association survive endogeneity checks (longer lags, first differences, reverse precedence)?

**Method — secondary research.** This is quantitative analysis of existing datasets; no primary data collection is involved. Sources: **BACI/CEPII** (bilateral trade, HS2, 2015–2024), **World Bank WGI and WDI** (governance, GDP), and **CEPII GeoDist** (distance, contiguity, language, colonial ties). Techniques: Balassa RCA, shift-share decomposition (Tyszynski 1951), and panel OLS gravity models with year effects and country-clustered standard errors (Tinbergen 1962; Santos Silva & Tenreyro 2006), plus an endogeneity battery (lag t−2, first differences, reverse precedence).

**Preliminary results (already computed on real data, October 2026).** The data pipeline is operational and the main findings exist:

- **Shift-share decomposition, verified to the dollar:** of Peru's +US$39.4bn export growth, **40% world demand, 19% product structure, 41% real competitiveness** (+US$16.1bn) — Peru genuinely gained market share.
- **World gravity model (184 partners, 1,496 observations, R² = 0.72):** lagged WGI is **positive and strongly significant (β = 0.667, p < 0.001)**; all standard gravity controls behave as theory predicts. A one-standard-deviation governance advantage (0.88 WGI points, the SD in the estimation panel) is associated with **~80% higher bilateral exports** (correct log-level transformation).
- **Design contrast as a methodological finding:** the same hypothesis tested on the 12-country CPTPP+China panel (N = 108) yields no significant effect — a power problem, not a data problem. This contrast offers a parsimonious explanation for the contradictory results in the literature.
- **All six WGI dimensions individually positive and significant** in the gravity model; regulatory quality (β = 0.681) and government effectiveness (β = 0.671) are the strongest — the most policy-actionable dimensions for trade facilitation.
- **Honest endogeneity assessment:** the association survives a t−2 lag but not first differences, and reverse precedence is significant. The white paper will report the finding as a robust **association**, consistent with the literature, and will **not** claim causality — an honest reporting standard that most comparable studies omit.

**Policy relevance.** Findings speak directly to: (i) whether trade-agreement governance chapters and trade-facilitation programs should prioritise regulatory quality and government effectiveness; (ii) how export-promotion agencies (PromPerú-type) should weight institutional diagnosis in partner-market selection; and (iii) the ongoing trade-governance debate in CPTPP accession, where institutional capacity is part of the accession conversation for new members.
